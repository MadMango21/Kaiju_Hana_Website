SunsetEcstasy

A world frozen in the blazing heat of an eternal sunset. An original Arabian-fantasy boss fight built around a dual-stance combat system and a boss that reads how you play.

Melee actionBoss battleOriginal IPUnreal Engine 5

Overview

Sayah, a nomad armed with a transforming glaive and the magic of the sun and the lost moonlight, journeys to the temple of Alsaam, the scorpion god, to free the imprisoned soul of the fallen hero Karaam.

The game's unique selling point is the Sun & Moon system: a dual combat meter that rewards aggressive, varied play and builds toward a powerful Eclipse ultimate. Built by a 16-person team over twelve weeks, it's the project where the three Kyeju Hana founders first fought side by side.

The goal was an industry-ready vertical slice: an original world, a new combat system and a boss encounter that holds up under ten-plus weeks of playtesting.

Design notes

Behind the fight.

Making stance-switching mandatory

In most soulslikes, switching weapons never feels necessary. The Sun & Moon bar fixes that: you need both stances to deal maximum damage and charge the Eclipse ultimate. A week-eight revamp made defence stance-specific too. Sun gives you a block and a big retreating dodge; Moon gives you a grapple hook and a nimble weave. Switching became a real decision, inspired by how Nioh forces stance changes mid-fight.

Combat design

A boss that notices you

Reactive Combat was a pillar from week one. Karaam tracks an invisible aggression meter and adapts with buffs, debuffs, new attack patterns and tempo shifts. A player who camps at range is punished differently from one who charges in. It's a direct answer to bosses that never react to how you're playing.

Systems & AI design

Two phases, one escalation

Karaam fights with Meels, Persian clubs that need a long wind-up, which gives players a readable window to react. At 50% health he reveals a scorpion tail, switches to a greatsword and adds poison stabs and a tail slam. Just when you think you've read the fight, you have to relearn it under pressure.

Boss design

Modular AI in Unreal Engine 5

Karaam's AI uses Behaviour Trees and the Environment Query System. Each behaviour is an independent node, so attacks, buffs and phase transitions can be added, swapped or tuned without touching the rest of the tree. When playtests showed a pattern was too punishing, the fix stayed isolated. That modularity is what made rapid iteration possible.

Unreal Engine · AI

Getting the setting right

The arena draws directly on mosque architecture: minarets, ablution fountains, faceless statues and a mihrab. The contrast between the outer desert and the cosmic sky inside the arena pushes the fantasy forward while staying grounded in something real. It's a setting that's badly underrepresented in games, and getting it right mattered.
